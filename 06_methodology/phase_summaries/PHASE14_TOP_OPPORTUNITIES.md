# Phase 14 — Investigate the Top Opportunities

## Status
**COMPLETE — FIVE PRIORITY CASE STUDIES INVESTIGATED**

## Purpose
Phase 14 tests whether the demographic signals produced by Model V1 survive contact with local, cricket-specific evidence. Five cases were deliberately selected to represent different geographic systems rather than simply taking the national top five:

- Wyndham, Victoria — #1, Melbourne outer growth belt
- Blacktown, NSW — #3, western Sydney
- Ipswich, Queensland — #4, South East Queensland
- Armadale, Western Australia — #10, Perth growth belt
- Maitland, NSW — #20, regional comparator

This phase does not create a formal System Readiness score. Local evidence is assessed qualitatively because nationally consistent club/program capacity data is still unavailable.

## 1. Wyndham — strongest validation case
Model V1 rank: #1
Opportunity score: 99.27
Age 5–14 in 2024: 56,395
2019–24 increase: +13,313 (+30.9%)

Local evidence materially validates the model signal. Cricket Victoria reported in July 2025 that overall cricket participation in Wyndham had increased 8% year-on-year and women/girls participation 18%. Wyndham City has separately installed lighting intended to increase carrying capacity at sporting facilities, and some council facility pages warn of constrained evening/weekend availability because of existing users.

**Working interpretation:** The demographic opportunity is not merely theoretical. Cricket participation is already growing, and there are signs that facility capacity is an active planning issue.

**Priority question:** Are club, oval, net and entry-program capacities expanding fast enough in the western growth areas of Wyndham?

## 2. Blacktown — strongest capacity-gap case
Model V1 rank: #3
Opportunity score: 97.21
Age 5–14 in 2024: 66,024
2019–24 increase: +9,571 (+17.0%)

Blacktown City Council stated in August 2024 that requests for year-round cricket competitions could not be met because of a shortage of fields. Cricket NSW's 2025/26 awards also recognised Blacktown City & District Cricket Association's Under-9s Transition Competition as the state's Junior Initiative winner.

**Working interpretation:** Blacktown combines a very large and growing junior market with a functioning junior-cricket ecosystem, but there is direct local-government evidence of cricket-field scarcity.

**Priority question:** Which growth catchments have the largest mismatch between junior population expansion and playable field capacity?

## 3. Ipswich — growth plus new supply
Model V1 rank: #4
Opportunity score: 96.58
Age 5–14 in 2024: 39,679
2019–24 increase: +4,829 (+13.9%)

Ipswich City Council projects very rapid long-run population growth. In April 2026 it sought expressions of interest from cricket/AFL users for a new South Ripley sports facility. Queensland Cricket separately reported that Strollers Cricket Club in Ipswich had grown to more than 110 junior players and Blasters, and that new cricket grounds had been established in Springfield.

**Working interpretation:** Unlike Blacktown's explicit shortage signal, Ipswich shows an ecosystem where new sporting capacity is actively being added while junior cricket is also growing.

**Priority question:** Can new facilities in Ripley/Springfield be paired with Blast, junior and girls' programs quickly enough to capture demographic growth?

## 4. Armadale — promising but needs participation validation
Model V1 rank: #10
Opportunity score: 93.65
Age 5–14 in 2024: 17,292
2019–24 increase: +3,696 (+27.2%)

Armadale's strength comes more from youth intensity and growth than national-scale market size. The City forecasts substantial long-run population growth. WA Cricket has reported a major pavilion upgrade at Gwynne Park, home to Armadale Cricket Club in summer, including four all-gender changerooms and improved club amenity.

**Working interpretation:** Infrastructure investment aligns with the demographic signal, but direct recent LGA-level participation evidence is weaker than for Wyndham, Blacktown or Ipswich.

**Priority question:** Is organised cricket participation growing at a comparable pace to Armadale's junior population, and is cricket provision distributed adequately beyond the principal Gwynne Park hub?

## 5. Maitland — useful regional comparator
Model V1 rank: #20
Opportunity score: 86.99
Age 5–14 in 2024: 13,946
2019–24 increase: +1,481 (+11.9%)

Maitland City Council began work in 2025 on a $10 million first sportsground for Chisholm, explicitly responding to growth and including a turf cricket wicket. Cricket NSW also highlighted a six-week all-girls clinic for 7–14 year olds in Maitland, while both Maitland senior and junior district associations sit within the NSW community competition structure.

**Working interpretation:** Maitland demonstrates that the model is not only detecting metropolitan opportunities. Here population growth, new infrastructure and targeted girls' programming are all visible.

**Priority question:** Can new infrastructure in Chisholm and surrounding growth areas translate into sustained junior and female registrations rather than one-off program activity?

## Cross-case finding
The five cases suggest three different patterns:

### Pattern A — Demand growth + capacity pressure
**Wyndham, Blacktown**

Demographic growth is accompanied by strong cricket activity, while infrastructure/capacity pressure is visible.

### Pattern B — Demand growth + capacity expansion
**Ipswich, Maitland**

Population growth is being met with new sporting infrastructure and/or targeted cricket programs.

### Pattern C — Demographic opportunity + incomplete participation evidence
**Armadale**

The demographic and infrastructure case is strong, but local cricket participation needs stronger validation before recommending major acquisition investment.

## Most important Phase 14 insight
The Model V1 ranking is useful as a screening mechanism, but the case studies show why it cannot be the final decision rule.

Two equally high opportunity areas can represent very different management situations:
- **Blacktown:** the issue may be constrained supply.
- **Ipswich:** new capacity may create an immediate activation opportunity.
- **Armadale:** demand itself needs stronger validation.

Therefore the next stage of a production-quality CA model should combine Opportunity Potential with direct internal evidence such as registrations, club capacity, program locations, facility utilisation and participant conversion.

## Recommended shortlist for deeper portfolio profiles
1. **Wyndham** — strongest all-round flagship case.
2. **Blacktown** — strongest apparent opportunity/capacity-gap case.
3. **Ipswich** — strongest growth-plus-new-capacity comparison.
4. **Armadale** — valuable test of whether the model over-predicts without participation evidence.
5. **Maitland** — regional comparator and women/girls program case.

## Phase 14 conclusion
Local evidence broadly strengthens rather than undermines the demographic opportunity model, but it also demonstrates that **opportunity is not the same as readiness**. The most valuable next information is no longer more demographic scoring—it is cricket-specific supply, participation and capacity data.
