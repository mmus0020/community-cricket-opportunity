# Phase 13 — Rank and Segment Opportunities

## Project
**Where Are Australia's Next Community Cricketers?**

## Status
**COMPLETE — OPPORTUNITY RANKING AND STRATEGIC SEGMENTATION**

## Purpose
Phase 12 produced a ranked national Opportunity Potential score. Phase 13 converts that score into decision types based on **why** an LGA ranks highly.

The segmentation remains demographic-only. It does not yet use cricket club/program readiness.

## Opportunity tiers
LGAs are grouped by Balanced Model score:

- **Tier 1 — Very High:** score >= 90
- **Tier 2 — High:** 80–89.99
- **Tier 3 — Moderate-High:** 65–79.99
- **Tier 4 — Moderate:** 50–64.99
- **Tier 5 — Lower Relative:** below 50

These are **relative national prioritisation tiers**, not claims of actual cricket demand.

## Strategic segments
The model uses transparent pillar rules rather than an opaque clustering algorithm.

### Balanced High-Opportunity Market
Very high Market Size, Youth Intensity and Growth Momentum.

Interpretation: strong scale, young structure and momentum simultaneously.

### Large Growth Market
High current market size and high growth, but youth concentration is less exceptional.

Interpretation: large acquisition pool with strong momentum.

### Emerging Young Growth Market
High youth concentration and high growth, but current market size is not yet among the largest nationally.

Interpretation: early intervention opportunity where growth may outpace existing services.

### Young High-Intensity Market
High youth concentration without equally strong scale or recent growth.

Interpretation: demographically young market requiring demand validation.

### Large Established / Slower-Growth Market
Large current junior market but weaker growth momentum.

Interpretation: likely stronger case for retention, conversion and utilisation than pure acquisition.

### Rapid Growth Market
High growth momentum with smaller current scale.

Interpretation: monitor and develop entry pathways before the market becomes larger.

### Mixed / Moderate Opportunity
No single dominant profile.

Interpretation: selective investigation rather than broad prioritisation.

## Why segmentation improves the ranking
A single score can make two LGAs look similar even when the underlying business problem differs.

For example:
- one LGA can rank highly because it already has a very large junior population;
- another can rank similarly because it is smaller but growing exceptionally quickly;
- another may combine high youth concentration with moderate scale.

These should not automatically receive the same participation intervention.

## Growth corridor comparison
The major geographic systems identified in Phase 11 show different pillar profiles rather than being interchangeable:
- Melbourne's outer belt combines very high youth concentration and growth momentum;
- western/south-west Sydney combines strong scale with major growth;
- South East Queensland has exceptional current market scale but a more mixed growth profile;
- Perth's growth belt shows strong growth and youth-intensity signals with somewhat smaller absolute market scale.

This supports corridor-level planning in addition to individual-LGA ranking.

## Management interpretation
Phase 13 creates the first practical decision language for the project:

### Very High / Balanced High-Opportunity
**Investigate first.**
Test cricket participation, club/program supply and capacity.

### Large Growth Market
**Scale acquisition if capacity exists.**
Look for school, Blast and club expansion potential.

### Emerging Young Growth Market
**Build ahead of demand.**
Investigate whether clubs and facilities are keeping pace with population growth.

### Large Established / Slower-Growth
**Prioritise retention and conversion.**
Pure acquisition may be less urgent than keeping existing participants engaged.

### Rapid Growth Market
**Monitor and seed delivery.**
Small current markets can become future capacity issues.

### Mixed / Moderate
**Use local evidence.**
Prioritise selectively rather than nationally.

## Important limitation
These segments are not yet the final Phase 6 strategic categories of:
- Activate / Accelerate;
- Build Capacity;
- Optimise Existing System;
- Monitor / Selective Investment.

Those final categories require the separate **Cricket System Readiness** dimension.

Phase 13 therefore segments only the **opportunity side** of the framework.

## Phase 13 conclusion
The ranking is now substantially more actionable because it identifies **different types of demographic opportunity** rather than treating every high-scoring LGA as the same market.

The next phase can select representative priority areas for deeper case-study investigation.
