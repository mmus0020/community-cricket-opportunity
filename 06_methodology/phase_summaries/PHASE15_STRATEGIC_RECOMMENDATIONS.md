# Phase 15 — Strategic Recommendations

## Project
**Where Are Australia's Next Community Cricketers?**

## Status
**COMPLETE — EVIDENCE-BASED STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS**

## 1. Core recommendation
Cricket Australia should use the public-data Opportunity Potential model as a **national screening tool**, not as a direct investment rule.

The recommended decision process is:

**Opportunity screening → Cricket readiness diagnosis → Problem classification → Intervention → Outcome measurement**

This protects against the central limitation identified throughout the project: demographic opportunity is not the same thing as actual cricket demand or system capacity.

## 2. Recommended strategic response framework

### A. High Opportunity + Capacity Pressure
**Response: Build Capacity First / Protect Access**

Use when demographic opportunity is high and local evidence indicates field, facility or program constraints.

Actions:
- audit field, net, club and program capacity;
- quantify unmet demand and waitlists;
- examine school/shared-facility access;
- expand lighting/usable hours where appropriate;
- avoid launching acquisition campaigns that the local system cannot absorb.

Primary examples: **Blacktown, Wyndham**.

### B. High Opportunity + New/Available Capacity
**Response: Activate / Accelerate**

Use where demographic opportunity is high and new or spare delivery capacity exists.

Actions:
- expand Cricket Blast and junior entry programs;
- create school-to-club pathways;
- launch girls-specific recruitment alongside new facilities;
- ensure new venues have club/program operators;
- track entry-to-club conversion.

Primary examples: **Ipswich, Maitland**.

### C. High Demographic Opportunity + Weak Demand Evidence
**Response: Validate Before Scaling**

Use where Model V1 ranks an area highly but direct cricket participation evidence remains limited.

Actions:
- obtain registration history;
- map club/program coverage;
- survey local clubs and schools;
- run small pilots;
- compare participation growth with demographic growth before major capital investment.

Primary example: **Armadale**.

### D. Large Market + Slower Growth
**Response: Retain / Convert**

Use where current junior-market scale is large but demographic momentum is weak.

Actions:
- prioritise year-to-year retention;
- analyse churn;
- improve Blast-to-junior conversion;
- investigate participant experience;
- optimise existing club/program assets.

### E. Small/Medium Market + Rapid Growth
**Response: Seed and Monitor**

Use where growth is fast but current scale remains modest.

Actions:
- establish low-cost entry programs;
- develop school partnerships;
- protect future facility capacity;
- monitor the market annually;
- avoid premature large capital commitments.

## 3. National recommendations

### N1 — Adopt two-stage national prioritisation
Use Opportunity Potential to identify where to investigate, then require participation/readiness evidence before resource allocation.

### N2 — Build a national Opportunity & Readiness dataset
Combine:
- PlayHQ registration history;
- club/program location;
- facility supply;
- participant age/gender;
- acquisition;
- retention/churn;
- local demographic growth.

The public prototype demonstrates what can be done without internal data. CA's internal systems could turn it into a much stronger operational model.

### N3 — Plan by growth corridor as well as LGA
Phase 11 found strong concentrations in:
- outer Melbourne;
- western/south-west Sydney;
- South East Queensland;
- Perth growth areas.

Participation infrastructure and players cross municipal borders. Corridor-level planning may therefore outperform isolated council-by-council responses.

### N4 — Establish an annual junior-growth early-warning dashboard
Monitor:
- target-age population growth;
- registrations;
- club/team capacity;
- facilities;
- program availability.

Flag areas where junior population is growing materially faster than cricket participation or supply.

### N5 — Separate acquisition, conversion, retention and capacity
A single total-participation KPI hides fundamentally different business problems.

Report separately:
- first-time participants;
- returning participants;
- Blast-to-junior conversion;
- junior-to-older-age transitions;
- waiting lists/unmet demand;
- facility utilisation.

### N6 — Add women/girls and multicultural strategic overlays
Do this using direct registration and robust demographic evidence, not assumptions.

The national model should be capable of identifying:
- general junior growth opportunities;
- girls-specific opportunities;
- multicultural participation opportunities;
- intersections between these groups.

### N7 — Evaluate every targeted intervention
Each initiative should define:
- baseline;
- intended target population;
- intervention;
- expected outcome;
- follow-up period.

Success should mean sustainable participation or improved access—not simply the number of sessions delivered.

## 4. Case-specific recommendations

### Wyndham
**Strategy: Build Capacity First + Targeted Acquisition**

Conduct a western-Melbourne capacity audit across the high-growth corridor. Map current cricket grounds, club/team capacity, school facilities and entry programs against junior population growth.

Once capacity is understood, expand targeted Blast and girls programs where the system can absorb additional players.

### Blacktown
**Strategy: Capacity-Constrained Priority**

Blacktown should be treated as a priority diagnostic case because local-government evidence already points to cricket-field shortage.

Quantify:
- unmet team demand;
- allocated field hours;
- field utilisation;
- travel distance;
- rapidly growing junior catchments.

### Ipswich
**Strategy: Activate / Accelerate**

Use new Ripley/Springfield sporting capacity as acquisition hubs.

Pair new facilities with:
- school recruitment;
- Cricket Blast;
- junior club pathways;
- girls programs.

The key is synchronising infrastructure delivery with participation programming.

### Armadale
**Strategy: Validate Before Scaling**

Before major investment, obtain direct evidence on:
- registration trends;
- local program distribution;
- club capacity;
- school interest.

Run targeted pilots if necessary.

A high demographic score should trigger research, not automatic investment.

### Maitland
**Strategy: Regional Growth Activation**

Use Chisholm's new cricket-capable infrastructure as an anchor for regional junior and girls acquisition.

Track whether participants move from clinics/school programs into sustainable club registrations.

## 5. Recommended KPI framework

### Acquisition
- new participant count;
- first-time registration rate.

### Retention
- season-to-season return rate;
- churn.

### Conversion
- Blast-to-junior club transition;
- school-to-club transition.

### Capacity
- participants/teams per playable cricket field-hour;
- program waitlists;
- team applications unable to be accommodated.

### Access
- travel distance/time to age-appropriate and gender-appropriate cricket.

### Inclusion
- girls acquisition/retention;
- priority-community registrations using appropriately collected data.

### Program effectiveness
- pilot participant → ongoing registration conversion.

### Growth alignment
- cricket-registration growth relative to target-age population growth.

## 6. Recommended management decision rule
A high Opportunity Potential score should trigger the question:

> **What prevents this area's demographic opportunity from becoming sustainable cricket participation?**

Possible answers:
1. Nothing substantial — **Activate / Accelerate**
2. Facility/program shortage — **Build Capacity First**
3. Weak evidence of cricket demand — **Validate Before Scaling**
4. Existing large market but weaker growth — **Retain / Convert**
5. Future growth not yet large enough — **Seed and Monitor**

## 7. Highest-priority recommendations for this portfolio project
If only three recommendations are featured in the eventual executive summary, use:

### 1. Create an Opportunity × Readiness national prioritisation system
This is the central strategic contribution.

### 2. Prioritise growth-corridor capacity planning
Especially outer Melbourne, western/south-west Sydney, South East Queensland and Perth growth areas.

### 3. Make registration conversion and system capacity explicit KPIs
This shifts reporting from 'how many play?' toward 'where are we acquiring, losing, converting or failing to accommodate players?'

## 8. Limit on recommendations
No recommendation in this project claims that a specific new facility, budget allocation or participant target will deliver a quantified return.

Those decisions require:
- internal CA registration data;
- detailed local facility utilisation;
- cost estimates;
- club capacity;
- stakeholder consultation.

## Phase 15 conclusion
The project now moves beyond identifying high-growth areas.

Its strategic recommendation is:

> **Use demographic opportunity to decide where to investigate, cricket-system evidence to diagnose the local constraint, and intervention-specific KPIs to determine what action actually works.**

This turns the analysis from a demographic ranking into a practical community-cricket decision framework.
